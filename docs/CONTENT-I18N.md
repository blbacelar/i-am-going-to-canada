# Content and internationalization

## Language requirement

Every public interface and page must be complete in:

- English (`en`)
- French (`fr`)
- Portuguese (`pt`)

This includes navigation, buttons, errors, form labels, alt text, metadata, structured data, legal notices and booking handoff copy.

## Content hierarchy

1. Company promise
2. Visitor need
3. Service explanation
4. Consultant fit
5. Trust evidence
6. Booking action

Marina's founder story can support credibility, but it must not dominate the homepage or make the other consultants feel secondary.

## Tone

- Clear, direct and reassuring.
- Professional without legal jargon.
- Human without false intimacy.
- Confident without certainty claims.
- Helpful without pressure.

## Prohibited language

Do not use claims equivalent to:

- guaranteed approval;
- your visa is assured;
- we know your chances;
- Canada needs you;
- the easiest immigration route;
- act now before it is too late;
- this consultant is the best for you;
- you qualify based on this website flow.

## Translation rules

- Use Canadian English and Canadian French.
- Portuguese should be natural Brazilian Portuguese unless the business specifies otherwise.
- Do not translate professional designations or regulated titles literally without review.
- Preserve official names and licence terminology.
- Do not use machine translation as the final legal or professional copy.
- French typography must use correct punctuation and spacing conventions.
- Avoid gendered assumptions about visitors.
- Test copy in layout rather than translating after design completion.

## Factual content workflow

For every factual claim:

1. Identify the current-site or owner-provided source.
2. Record whether it is verified.
3. Translate only after the source text is approved.
4. Use `TODO_CONTENT` where information is missing.
5. Obtain final owner review for credentials, services, policies and disclaimers.

## Testimonials

- Use only genuine, approved testimonials.
- Keep original meaning across translations.
- Identify anonymization honestly.
- Do not add outcome claims that were not stated.
- Obtain consent before displaying a name, image or case detail.

## Legal disclaimer starter

Prepare localized, professionally reviewed wording that communicates:

- website information is general;
- it is not individual legal or immigration advice;
- viewing the site or using the concierge does not create a professional-client relationship;
- an engagement begins only under the firm's approved process and agreement.

The Codex must not invent final legal wording. Mark it `TODO_LEGAL_REVIEW` until approved.
