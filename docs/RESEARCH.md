# Research and audit brief

## Current website

Primary source: [iamgoingtocanada.ca](https://iamgoingtocanada.ca/)

The Codex must inspect the live site before implementation. Capture facts rather than copying the old interface.

Audit:

- pages and URLs;
- current services;
- consultant names, biographies and credentials;
- Calendly and contact links;
- existing legal pages;
- logo and brand assets;
- testimonials and consent implications;
- article/resource URLs with SEO value;
- navigation and mobile behavior;
- accessibility and performance issues;
- analytics tags and third-party embeds;
- content that centers Marina instead of the team.

Create a source ledger with three statuses:

- `verified-current`
- `needs-owner-confirmation`
- `do-not-migrate`

## Competitor/reference set

Review current versions of at least six relevant sites. The following list is a starting set; verify URLs and business relevance before citing:

- Moving2Canada

Also study two or three non-immigration references with excellent professional-service journeys, multilingual UX or team-selection experiences.

## Comparison dimensions

| Dimension | Questions |
| --- | --- |
| Positioning | Is the firm or an individual the primary brand? |
| Hero | Is the value proposition clear within seconds? |
| Navigation | Can visitors find services and people without jargon? |
| Team | Are consultants comparable and equally credible? |
| Conversion | What path leads to booking? |
| Trust | How are regulation, credentials, process and testimonials presented? |
| Multilingual UX | Are languages complete, discoverable and indexable? |
| Content | Does the site educate without overwhelming? |
| Visual identity | What feels distinctive versus templated? |
| Mobile | Is the primary action easy on a phone? |
| Accessibility | Are controls, contrast and semantics credible? |
| Performance | Do imagery, video or booking widgets create friction? |

## Strategic hypothesis to test

Most immigration sites either feel institutional and text-heavy or personality-led and dependent on one consultant. I Am Going To Canada can differentiate by combining:

- a visible, multilingual consultant team;
- a calm concierge that reduces choice overload;
- editorial visual quality;
- transparent boundaries around what the website can and cannot advise;
- a direct, low-friction path to the correct Calendly.

Research should validate or refine this hypothesis rather than merely confirm it.
