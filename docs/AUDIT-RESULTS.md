# Audit results

Research date: 2026-08-20. Sources were reviewed for information architecture, public content, conversion patterns and current URLs. Findings are evidence for a new project-specific system, not permission to copy another site's design or copy.

## Executive findings

The current site contains useful factual seeds but does not yet express I Am Going To Canada as a multilingual team practice. Its homepage is branded and written primarily around Marina, while the team and booking pages reveal a broader organization only after navigation. The highest-value opportunity is to make the team visible immediately and replace broad reading and sensitive inquiry forms with a short, non-diagnostic route to a professional.

The redesign should preserve verified consultant facts and booking destinations, but replace the current website-builder structure, generic SEO, mixed-language pages and personality dependency. It should also remove the homepage form that asks for age, language level, education, professional experience, marital status, children and citizenship/residence; those fields exceed what a public navigation funnel needs and create unnecessary privacy risk.

## Current brand and technical signals

- The rendered business name is “Marina Snyder Immigration Consultant”, while the new product commitment is I Am Going To Canada.
- No distinctive production logo was found in the rendered header; the PWA references a website-builder default icon. Use a typographic wordmark until an owner-approved logo is supplied.
- Current theme colour is bright red (`#d30000`) and the builder uses League Spartan. Neither forms a sufficiently complete brand system to preserve unchanged.
- The site is generated by GoDaddy Website Builder and ships large generated CSS/class output, third-party scripts, reCAPTCHA and consent tooling.
- Homepage metadata is generic and inconsistent with the page language and new company positioning.
- The current site publishes a physical address, phone, email and social profiles; all must be owner-confirmed before production migration.

## Patterns worth adapting

1. Organize choices around the visitor's current need, then reveal professional detail progressively.
2. Keep a visible, stable language control rather than treating languages as unrelated pages.
3. Present trust through verified regulated titles, process transparency and scope boundaries—not large invented counters.
4. Make consultant comparison consistent: portrait, role, languages, service categories, profile and booking.
5. Keep the primary booking action visible, but never force a visitor to pick a consultant before understanding the team.
6. Use direct service families with plain-language descriptions and defer program-level detail until confirmed.

## Patterns to avoid

- Personality-first hero composition.
- Eligibility quizzes disguised as lead-generation flows.
- Mega-menus that expose every immigration program at once.
- Sensitive intake questions in a public, unauthenticated funnel.
- Generic “Canadian dream”, urgency and success language.
- Unverified counters, ratings, testimonials, credentials or award badges.
- Separate translated pages with inconsistent navigation and metadata.
- Repeated equal cards for every section and decorative Canada clichés.

## Recommended information architecture

Every route is localized under `/en`, `/fr` and `/pt`:

- Home
- Find a consultant
- Consultants
  - Consultant profile by slug
- Services
  - Service detail by slug when content is confirmed
- About
- FAQ
- Privacy
- Disclaimer

The root route redirects to `/en` without geolocation. The locale switcher preserves the equivalent route where possible. Sitemap and structured data are generated from the same validated data that renders the directory.

## Conversion hypothesis

If the first viewport presents the company as a multilingual team and immediately offers a calm “find your consultant” action, visitors will reach relevant profiles and verified Calendly links with less reading and less uncertainty than the current services-first, Marina-led flow. The concierge should ask only preferred language and broad support category, show why each profile appears, and always expose the full team.

## SEO migration risks

- Current language URLs use `/english`, `/português` and `/français`; redirects are required to `/en`, `/pt` and `/fr`.
- Generic page titles and mixed language metadata need full replacement.
- `/our-team` and `/book-a-consultation` need localized successors and redirects.
- Published profile/service text may currently attract branded searches; preserve factual meaning while improving hierarchy.
- Do not index a review preview. Production canonical URLs must remain `https://iamgoingtocanada.ca` only after approval.
- `/blog` contains no rendered articles, but verify search-console data before removing or redirecting it.
